# VO-AI — 2 Minute Video Narration

**Just the words.** 308 words. At a natural speaking pace of roughly 150 words per minute
that is about **2 minutes 3 seconds** — marginally over. Read briskly and it fits; if you
prefer to read slowly, drop the [1:10] paragraph, which takes it to about 1 minute 48.

Time markers show where you should be, so you can tell mid-recording whether you are running
fast or slow.

---

## The narration

**[0:00]**

On a construction project, one instruction — change the floor tile from ceramic to marble —
can take a quantity surveyor hours. Check the contract. Find the clause. Compare every rate
against the bills of quantities. Miss one, and someone is paid the wrong amount.

**[0:14]**

VO-AI manages that process for the three parties who share it — contractor, consultant, client.
Each sees the same register, and each edits only their own columns.

**[0:26]**

As the consultant, I see what is waiting on me — and what is already late. The contract gives
me thirty days to evaluate a variation. VO-AI tracks that clock.

**[0:38]**

This is the heart of it. For every measured item, VO-AI compares the rate the contractor
claimed against the rate in the priced contract bills.

The omitted ceramic tiling matches the contract rate. Same rate.

The marble has no comparable item in the bills. It is flagged as a star rate, to be agreed
separately.

And the skirting is claimed at thirty-one ringgit, against a contract rate of twenty-two.
VO-AI states which rate governs, and by how much the claim is overstated. That single line is
worth over fifteen hundred ringgit.

**[1:10]**

Changes also spread. Change a block wall to brick, and VO-AI asks whether the wall finishes,
the damp-proof course and the skirting need remeasuring too.

**[1:22]**

Every variation is matched to its governing clause from the PAM and PWD standard forms, with
the evidence required — then a draft variation order report, ready to print.

**[1:34]**

The client sees the same facts, put for a decision: what changed, what it is worth, and what
it adds up to against the contract sum.

**[1:43]**

Every figure you have seen is computed from the data entered. VO-AI shows no confidence score
it cannot justify, and cites no clause it cannot find. Where it is unsure, it says so.

Decision support — with the professional still deciding.

**[1:52 — end]**

## If you need to cut further

Drop the **[1:10]** paragraph about block walls. It is the most interesting feature after the
rate check, but it is the only one the video can lose without breaking the argument. That
takes you to roughly 1 minute 40.

Never cut from **[0:38]**. That section is the video.

## Reading notes

**Pace.** Two minutes feels short and the temptation is to rush. Don't. The 0:38 section is
the whole point; if you rush it, the video fails even if everything else is perfect. Read
that part slower than the rest.

**The three verdicts.** Pause between "Same rate", the star rate, and the skirting. Three
findings, three beats. Let each land.

**Numbers.** Say "thirty-one ringgit" and "twenty-two", not "RM thirty-one". Spoken, the
currency code interrupts the sentence.

**The last line.** Say it plainly — no emphasis, no rising tone. It is the most credible
sentence in the video and it does not need help.

**What not to say.** Avoid "AI-powered", "revolutionary", "cutting-edge", "seamless". The
audience is construction professionals; a demonstrated control beats any adjective. Notice
the script never claims intelligence — it shows a rate being checked.

**Subtitles.** Recommended by the submission rules. If you subtitle in Chinese, switch the
app to 中文 for one shot around 1:34 so the bilingual interface is visible rather than
merely claimed.

**Recording.** Record the screen silently first, then narrate over it. Clicking and talking
at once is how you end up with twelve takes.
